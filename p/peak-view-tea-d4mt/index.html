1670ft

We believe we are the highest tearoom in England.

Peak View sits 1,670 feet above sea level on the A537, so the views across the Peaks are astounding. In fine weather the outdoor seating takes the whole of it in.

Stunning sunsets, from the tearoom’s own picture gallery.

The way up is the A537.

From Macclesfield, the tea rooms are about 8 miles out into the Peak Park, on the left of the main road, set up on the hill.

From Buxton, go past the Cat & Fiddle pub and the tea rooms are on your right as you start to go back down the hill towards Macclesfield.

No car? Take the number 58 bus from Macclesfield or Buxton and ask to be dropped off at the tearoom.

Home cooked, to order.

Built in 1956, still on the hill.

  1. 1956

    The Moorland Café is built

    Mrs Winifred Ivy and John Mason put up the original tearoom. The fireplace stone came from Errwood Hall, where John Mason had been a footman and gamekeeper. A Buxton artist painted the café in 1958, and the painting, bought and restored, hangs in the tearoom.

  2. Next

    The Dish and Spoon

    The name most people still remember it by. In 1973 the Macclesfield Express Advertiser reported a school minibus driver dropping children off and finding a 250lb unexploded bomb. The owner, Albert Capper, called the police, who called the army bomb squad.

  3. Early 1980s

    The Shining Tor restaurant

    Alan Winchester bought the café, renovated it and built an extension from the stone of an old Yorkshire mill, using a local builder. The smaller downstairs room was the weekday café; the larger one opened on Sundays for restaurant meals.

  4. 1989 to 2007

    Two more owners

    Mr and Mrs Griffiths took it on at the end of 1989. Julia and Chris Bates bought it around 1998 or 1999 and built the business up until 2007.

  5. 2007

    Gail and Philip Heath

    The building keeps many of its original features, from the beams and the fireplace stone to the original Traser tiles, which were very expensive in the 1950s.

  6. 2013

    Extended and refurbished

    With improved facilities for less able-bodied visitors.
